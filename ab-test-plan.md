A/B Test Plan – College Closet

Date: November 9, 2026

1. Page and Element Being Tested

This experiment will test the main call-to-action button on the College Closet homepage.

Version A: The current button wording, “Explore Our Styles.”

Version B: The new button wording, “Shop College Outfits.”

Only the wording of the button will change. The button's placement, color, size, and the rest of the homepage will remain the same.

2. Hypothesis

I believe Version B will perform better because “Shop College Outfits” tells visitors exactly what they can do next. The wording is more direct and makes the shopping purpose of the website clear. I expect AI visitors to be more likely to say they would click the button and continue browsing the store.

3. AI Visitor and Test Prompt

The AI visitor will act as a college student looking for affordable and trendy outfits. The same AI model and settings will be used for every trial. I will record the exact model name and settings before beginning the experiment.

The following prompt will be used for both versions:

“You are a college student looking for affordable and trendy outfits. Visit the College Closet homepage and explore what the website offers. Based on the page, explain what you would do next and whether you would continue browsing the store. Give a short explanation of your decision.”

Each trial will use a new conversation. AI visitors will be randomly assigned to Version A or Version B. The prompt and all other testing conditions will stay the same.

4. Sample Size and Timing

I plan to collect at least 50 trials for Version A and 50 trials for Version B, for a minimum of 100 total trials. I will launch the test by November 17, 2026, and record the dates and results as I complete each group of trials.

5. Primary Outcome

The main outcome I will measure is the percentage of AI visitors who say they would click the homepage button or use it to continue shopping.

I will count a trial as a positive response when the AI visitor clearly indicates that they would use the button to move forward and browse the store. I will compare the percentage of positive responses for Version A with the percentage for Version B.

I will also keep track of comments about how clear and helpful the button wording is.

6. How I Will Compare Results

After collecting at least 50 trials for each version, I will calculate the percentage of positive responses for both versions and compare the results.

I will consider my hypothesis supported if Version B has a positive response rate at least 10 percentage points higher than Version A. If the difference is smaller or Version A performs better, I will not consider the hypothesis supported.

I will keep the raw results, AI responses, testing dates, and screenshots so I can explain how I reached my conclusion in the final report.
